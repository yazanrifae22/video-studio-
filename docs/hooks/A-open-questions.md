# A. Spoken and text open questions (round 3, 2026-11-03)

Scope: first-line questions, curiosity-gap lines, challenge captions, re-hook lines, honest promise wording, Syrian Arabic phrasing.
Out of scope: frame 0 and withheld motion (B), loops and endings (C).
Evidence level: weak. Almost all sources are practitioner blogs. Two peer-reviewed items exist (S1, S2) and they support moderate specificity, not "more mystery". Where a number below comes from a blog it is marked (blog). Treat all timings as hypotheses to test on our own retention graphs.

## Sources (all new, none from the banned list)
- S1 Scott, "You won't believe what's in this paper! Clickbait, relevance and the curiosity gap", Journal of Pragmatics 2021. https://eprints.kingston.ac.uk/47719 . A gap opens when the line names a new category and supplies little content. Relevance to the viewer is what makes it click.
- S2 CHI 2025, "Curious Shorts". https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713951 . Curiosity prompts on Shorts drive follow-up exploration. The nudges tested sit at the end of the video, not the opening.
- S3 1of10, "Ethical Clickbait". https://1of10.com/blog/the-definitive-guide-to-ethical-clickbait-how-to-lie-truthfully/ . Promise the most extreme TRUE moment. No loophole wording. Run a "would viewers feel cheated?" check. Soften with "almost" when needed.
- S4 Overseer OS, "Click Promise". https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-click-promise . Define the payoff in ONE sentence before writing the hook. Failures are payoff too late, too weak or not delivered. Open with the specific claim, not background.
- S5 Retensis, hook formulas. https://retensis.com/blog/best-hook-formulas-short-form . Unfinished story = open on the peak, cut before resolution. Challenge formula "I bet you can't...". Rotate your top 3-4 formulas. Track the first 3-5 s.
- S6 Prepublish, Shorts retention. https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-shorts-retention . Value in the first 2 s, a new beat every 2-3 s, payoff at about 70-77% of runtime, hook reminder in the last 5-6 s (blog).
- S7 Zella, pattern interrupts. https://zellahq.com/blog/pattern-interrupts-video-retention/ . A change at least every 3-6 s (blog, no data).
- S8 Pixflow/Prepublish interrupts roundup (via search): the only source that uses "micro open loops every 5-8 s". These are spoken teasers, not visual changes. https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-pattern-interrupts
- S9 Submagic Arabic hook library. https://www.submagic.co/ar/blog/الافضل-hooks-لتيكتوك-وانستغرام . Arabic templates are translated MSA, e.g. "ماذا لو أخبرتك...", "هل تعلم أن...", "لا تكرهني ولكن...". They are NOT Syrian and read like machine translation. Use only as a pattern list.
- S10 SAN, YouTube clickbait enforcement, 2024-12-26. https://san.com/cc/youtube-cracks-down-on-clickbait-with-new-enforcement-measures/ . Title or thumbnail that promises something the video does not deliver gets the video removed (no strike at first).
- S11 "Viewer Responses to Clickbait on YouTube: A Marketing Perspective". https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395261047 . Promise-delivery mismatch leaves viewers dissatisfied.
- S12 Go-Viral hook formulas. https://www.go-viral.app/blog/best-hooks-for-short-form-video/ . Template list ("the reason X is not what you think"). Anecdotal.
- S13 Transparent Language blog, Levantine question words. https://blogs.transparent.com/arabic/?p=18713 . Colloquial question words (شو, ليش, وين, كيف, شلون).
- S14 Syriac Press, words in Syrian speech. https://syriacpress.com/?p=82409 . Confirms شو and ليش as everyday Syrian forms; شلون leans northern. Etymology claim is single-source.
GAP: I found no source of real Syrian-dialect hook lines by Arabic creators. The Levantine lines below are our own drafts. A native Syrian speaker must approve each one before use.

## Design rules that come out of the sources
- R-A1. One-sentence payoff first, hook second (S4). The hook is the payoff's question form.
- R-A2. Gap must be concrete and solvable. Moderate detail beats pure vagueness (S1, registered report cited in search). Name the thing, hide only the outcome.
- R-A3. Pay it inside the video, visibly (S3, S4). Default payoff window 70-77% of runtime (S6). For a 30-45 s Podium Pop reel, that is 22-33 s.
- R-A4. Never a loophole. If the true version is smaller, say "almost" or "nearly" (S3).

## Catalogue

### M1. Named-Outcome Question
What: first line names the subject and asks for the result. "Who has the hardest shot in this game?"
Why: concrete gap, solvable, relevant (S1, S3).
Recipe: voice starts at 0.0 s, the question is 6-9 words and ends by 1.8 s. Matching caption text item from 0.0 to 2.2 s, fade in 0.1 s. Answer withheld until at least rank 1. Needs [can] for text + audio; keep the caption in the top third.
Failure: the question names no subject ("You won't believe this") = empty gap, drop-off at 2 s. Also fails if the answer is already visible in the first frame.
Honesty test: can the answer be pointed to at a timestamp? Write "answer at T=__ s" into the hook record. No timestamp = bait.
- Podium Pop: "Who hit the hardest shot in history? Number one shocked me."  (Do NOT say "shocked me" unless the sound or visual of #1 is truly the strongest).
- Mission Voices: "What did the astronaut say when the hatch opened?" Pay with the real quote.
- Way It Was Made: "Why did this engine need four tries to start?"
- quiz-ar: "شو شايف بهالصورة؟" (draft). Pays at the reveal of each round.
- Tiko & Pip: "Where did Pip hide the red ball?" Answer within 20 s, no scare.

### M2. Most-Extreme-True-Moment Tease (unfinished story)
What: say the peak in plain words, cut before it resolves. "He did this from the halfway line... and then the keeper moved."
Why: S3 (promise the most extreme true thing) and S5 (open on peak, cut before resolution).
Recipe: 0.0-2.5 s voice states the peak, picture cuts at the last beat before contact, 2.5-3.0 s a pause of 0.4 s, then the build starts. Peak is paid at the reveal slot. [can]
Failure: the tease is bigger than the footage (a "miracle" that is a normal save). Viewer leaves at the payoff and the retention graph shows a cliff right after it.
Honesty test: show the peak to someone who has not seen the video. If the hook wording is stronger than the clip in their reaction, tone it down. Use "almost" / "nearly" if it was.
- Podium Pop: use a real near-miss ("one centimetre from the crossbar").
- Mission Voices: "He had four seconds to decide." Only if the log shows it.
- Way It Was Made: "The test rig held for 9 minutes. On minute 10 it did not." Only with a dated source.
- quiz-ar: not a fit (rounds are self-contained).
- Tiko & Pip: use a soft version ("Tiko almost reached the top") and pay within 10 s.

### M3. Challenge Caption ("Can you beat it?")
What: a direct dare as a caption or spoken line, with a fair test. "I bet you can't spot it in 3 seconds."
Why: challenge formula (S5). Cheap to compute: it invites a replay.
Recipe: caption 0.0-1.6 s, text pill, big. Visible timer or beat of 3 s [can]: three text items "3", "2", "1" at 1 s spacing, scale keyframes 1.0 to 1.15 in 0.2 s (ease-out). Reveal at 3.0 s. Per-word highlight [needs-eng: per-word karaoke].
Failure: impossible challenge (the viewer feels tricked) or one that is not actually fair (the peek-clue problem from the quiz lessons).
Honesty test: prove fairness: every partial clue tested against all options at 360 px width. If an answer cannot be seen on a fair watch, the challenge is bait.
- quiz-ar: «جرّب تلاقيها قبل ما يخلص العدّ» (draft). Best fit.
- Podium Pop: "Name this player in 2 seconds." Only for TOP 5 intros.
- Mission Voices: "Can you guess the mission by the voice?" Must name the mission at the end.
- Way It Was Made: "Guess the machine from its sound."
- Tiko & Pip: gentle version: "Can you spot the blue bird?" No countdown pressure, no "you can't".

### M4. Scripted Wrong-Guess (open question, then answer a different question)
What: the narrator asks a question, tells the obvious answer, and says "not quite" before the truth.
Why: curiosity needs a short gap (S1). A known wrong answer sharpens the right one.
Recipe: 0.0-2.0 s question, 2.0-3.5 s common guess on screen, 3.5-4.0 s cut or pause 0.3 s, true answer at the reveal. [can]
Failure: the "right" answer is not really different from the guess.
Honesty test: write both answers in the hook record. If they are the same fact reworded, delete the line.
- Podium Pop: "You'd say Messi. It isn't."  Only if the data shows it is not.
- Mission Voices / Way It Was Made: "Most people think X. The record says Y." Source must be named in script.json.

### M5. Re-Hook Line (spoken micro open loop)
What: a short line every 5-8 s that points to what is coming, not what has passed. "Number two has a twist." "Wait for the net."
Why: S8 uses 5-8 s for spoken loops; S6 and S7 want a change every 2-6 s. Rule: spoken teaser every 5-8 s plus a visual change every 2-4 s (visual changes belong to B).
Recipe for a 40 s reel: re-hook at about 8 s, 15 s, 22 s, 30 s. Each is 3-6 words, placed in the silent beat BEFORE the next reveal, voice duck on the music by 0.3 s [can: volume keyframes]. One caption pill per re-hook: fade 0.1 s in, 0.3 s out. Max 4 per video.
Failure: every line says "wait for it" with nothing different behind it. The voice becomes noise and viewers stop trusting it.
Honesty test: every re-hook points to a specific next beat that exists. Log the timestamp of the beat each one points to. If none exists, delete the line.
- Podium Pop: "Number three is worse than you think. Wait." Pay at the next reveal.
- Mission Voices: "Listen to what he says next." Cut to the line. Never replace the quote.
- Way It Was Made: "Here is the part nobody filmed twice."
- quiz-ar: «التالتة أصعب» (draft). Pays with a harder round, not an easier one.
- Tiko & Pip: "Next we meet Pip's friend!" No tension, just anticipation.

### M6. Number Gap
What: a specific number with the thing it measures hidden. "Number one is 11 metres long."
Why: numbers make the gap concrete (S1, S12).
Recipe: show the number in a text item at 0.0-2.0 s, scale 0.9 to 1.0 over 0.25 s [can]. Say the number once. Hide what it measures until the reveal.
Failure: made-up or rounded-for-effect numbers.
Honesty test: every number must have a source line in script.json (claim to evidence, as in showrunner-base).
- Mission Voices: "28 seconds of silence on that call." Only if logged.
- Way It Was Made: "1,200 parts, no two the same." Source required.
- Podium Pop: "Ball speed: 131 km/h" from a real source.
- Tiko & Pip: counting items ("Find all 5 ducks").

### M7. Direct Wrong-Belief Question (contrarian)
What: "Everyone says X. Is it true?" S5, S12.
Recipe: 0.0-2.2 s on voice + caption, answer at the video's final third (S6, 70%).
Failure: the video agrees with the belief, so the gap was fake.
Honesty test: the title claim must survive "what about X?". Do a counter-example search before using it.
- Way It Was Made / Mission Voices: fits history myths with sources.
- Podium Pop: only for opinions flagged as opinion.
- quiz-ar and Tiko & Pip: not a fit (Tiko must stay gentle, avoid "wrong").

## Arabic (Syrian) phrasing, draft bank for quiz-ar
All need native-speaker approval before use (see GAP). Pattern from S9 translated into dialect. Question words from S13/S14: شو, ليش, وين, كيف.
- Open question: «شو شايف؟» (brand line). «شو لقيت بالصورة؟» «شو الغلط هون؟»
- Challenge: «بتلاقيها قبل ما يخلص الوقت؟» «جرّب وما تغش.»
- Number gap: «في ٣ أشياء مخبّاية.»  (Only if exactly 3.)
- Re-hook: «الجاية أصعب.» «لسا في مفاجأة.» (Need a real harder round or a real surprise.)
- Pay line: «شفتوها؟ هي هون.» (Say where the answer is, not just "it's easy").
Use MSA or colloquial consistently within a video. No "حدس"/intuition claims.
Do NOT use: «ما رح تصدق» / «صدمة» unless the footage truly shocks. These are the bait words.

## Wording a promise so it is paid honestly (checklist)
1. Write the payoff in one sentence BEFORE the hook (S4).
2. Hook = that sentence as a question, with one fact hidden. Hide the OUTCOME, never the SUBJECT.
3. Put "answer at T=__ s" in the hook record. Default 70-77% of runtime (S6).
4. Test with the mute glance: caption alone must state the promise.
5. Downgrade words when true value is smaller: "almost", "nearly", "one of". (S3)
6. Description and title must repeat the same promise, no bigger.
7. QA check: delete the hook line; if the video still makes sense and feels honest, the line was decoration, otherwise confirm the payoff exists. Fail if the promise is not paid on screen (S10, S11).

## Timeline recipe (copy for a 40 s Podium Pop reel)
- 0.0-2.0 s: M1 or M2 line, caption on at 0.0 s.
- 2.0-8.0 s: first clip, no new promise.
- ~8 s: re-hook 1 (M5). ~15 s, ~22 s, ~30 s: re-hooks 2-4 as needed.
- 28-33 s (70-77%): promise paid (rank 1 reveal). Silent beat 0.4 s after.
- Final 5 s: leave to section C.
