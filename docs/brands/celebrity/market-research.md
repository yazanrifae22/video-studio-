## 3. Rights and policy risk assessment (critical)

| Risk | What the policy says | Our exposure | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Copyright / Content ID** | Content ID "can't decide fair use". While a claim is active, monetization is typically blocked. Credit lines and "no infringement intended" do **not** create fair use. Fair use is weighed on 4 factors, and outside the US, fair dealing and EU exceptions are narrower (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148) | **Very high** if using TV, sports, music, or interview clips. Sports leagues and broadcasters (NBA, FIFA, NFL, MLB, F1) claim aggressively | **No third-party video or audio clips. No commercial music.** Use original animation and narration, licensed stock music, and original sound effects |
| **Reused content** (monetization) | Ineligible: "Clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative"; content "downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications"; reaction content with no added voice commentary. Applies **even with permission** and is assessed **channel-wide** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392) | High for typical "celebrity moments" compilation channels | 100% original narration and visuals. Each video has a thesis and analysis, not a recap |
| **Inauthentic content** (renamed 2025-07-15) | Ineligible: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates"; image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with little narrative value. YouTube says this is not a ban on AI (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 ; Search Engine Journal https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/) | **High** for an automation studio. "AI voice over a slideshow of celebrity photos" is the exact pattern targeted | Per-video custom illustration, varied structure, scripted human-edited narration, real research and citations on screen |
| **Altered / synthetic content disclosure** | Must disclose realistic content that makes "a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do". Clearly unrealistic animation does not need disclosure (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491) | Medium | Use **stylized, non-photoreal** caricature only. Never generate realistic likeness or voice clones of celebrities. Depict only documented events, labeled "dramatization" |
| **Defamation / privacy / right of publicity** | Not YouTube policy but legal. Real people can also file privacy complaints | Medium (higher with criminal or personal-life topics) | Stick to court records and reputable reporting, cited on screen and in the description. Label allegations as allegations. No health, sexuality, family, or minors topics. Never imply endorsement in thumbnails. Get legal review for criminal-case stories (Holmes) |
| **Downloaders (savefrom.net etc.)** | YouTube's Terms forbid downloading or reproducing content "except as expressly authorized by the Service" or with written permission (https://www.youtube.com/t/terms). A downloader is a tool, **not a license**. Possessing a file grants zero rights | High if used as a source | **Do not use savefrom.net or any downloader as a rights source.** Its own terms could not be fetched (blocked, **unverified**), but no downloader can grant copyright permission in any case |
| **Photos** | Getty/AP/Reuters photos are copyrighted. "Editorial use" licenses cost money and have terms. Press-kit and publicity images are usually licensed **only for editorial coverage of the specific project** | Medium | Default to **no photos**. Use press-kit images only when the kit's terms permit and the video covers that project; keep the source and terms in a rights log. Public-domain or CC images (Wikimedia Commons, with license check and attribution) are acceptable |

This is not legal advice. Have a media lawyer review the rights workflow before monetizing.

## 4. Recommended SAFE format: "Illustrated Story Essay"

- **Visuals:**
  - Original 2D illustrated, stylized caricatures (clearly not photoreal), with consistent art direction.
  - Animated timelines, data charts (stats from official league or award sources), maps, and kinetic typography of **short attributed quotes**, a sentence or two from published interviews with the outlet named on screen.
  - Optional press-kit stills under their terms. **Zero clips.**
- **Audio:**
  - Original scripted narration, either a human or a licensed, consented synthetic voice (never a celebrity voice clone).
  - Royalty-free licensed music.
- **Structure (long, 8–12 min):**
  - Cold-open "moment" (5–15 s)
  - Thesis ("This one night explains her whole career")
  - Three acts with dates and sources
  - The turning point
  - What it means now
  - Sources card
- **Structure (Shorts, 35–55 s):** one anecdote, one turn, one punchline, and a "sources in description" line.
- **Positioning:** **"The Moment That Changed Everything: animated stories of the turning points behind famous careers."** It is evergreen-friendly, works with the news calendar, and is positive or analytical rather than gossip. Its core is sports plus entertainment turning points, which are less legally sensitive than scandals.

## 5. Growth to the first 1,000 genuine views

1. **News-jack within 24–72 h** using the pageview spikes above. Publish while interest is peaking (Tyson doc week, Ballon d'Or on Oct 26, World Series from Oct 23).
2. **Shorts as the engine:** 1 per day, one anecdote each. Tony Doesn't Draw's top Shorts reach 1M+ while its long-form gets about 100K.
3. **Titles use proven patterns:**
   - "How X Became…"
   - "The Night X…"
   - "The Moment That Changed X"
   - "Why Everyone Is Talking About X Again"
   - "X vs Y: The Real Story"
4. **Thumbnails:** a stylized illustrated face (ours, not a photo), one emotion, one prop, 2–4 words.
5. **Hook (first 3 s):** start at the turning point ("September 12th. 2 sets down. 22,000 people…"), then rewind.
6. **Genuine sharing:** fan subreddits and forums (follow their rules), the X/Threads/Instagram art crop, and Discord fan servers. No bought views.
7. **Cadence:** 1 Short per day plus 2 long-form per week. Keep a "rights log" sheet for every asset.

## 6. Weighted idea-scoring model

**Score = 0.30·demand + 0.20·competitionGap + 0.20·visualPotential + 0.15·productionEase + 0.15·(6 − policyRisk)**, with every factor 1–5 and policyRisk at 5 = highest risk.
- Celebrity baseline policyRisk is 2.
- Add +1 for living-person scandal or crime, +1 for heavily claimed sports leagues (the temptation to use clips), and +1 for political figures.
- Do not ship anything at policyRisk ≥ 4 without legal review.
