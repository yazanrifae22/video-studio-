## 1. Diagnosis (our data: analytics 2026-11-07, `videos.csv`, n = 15, channel 5 subs)
- **Views:** median 102, mean 343, total 5,145. Top 3: M9.5 quake 1,359 (70.4% viewed), volcano plume ranking 1,202 (52.4%), winds ranking 1,170 (38.1%). Bottom 5: 17-31 views.
- **Traffic:** Shorts feed 4,751 of 5,147 (92%); search 347 (7%). The feed tested us and mostly said no; search is a small but real second door.
- **Confound (be honest):** 12 of 15 were published in 6 days (Oct 26-31, 2/day+), so ages differ and the two oldest-by-feed (Oct 25/26 lists) had the longest runway. The three leaders are NOT proof that lists win; the 20-30 view tail is not proof the topics are bad.
- **What the best did:** the quake video is the only top-3 video that is a single named Earth event with the number in the title (26 s). Retention is its edge (70% vs 52% and 38%). The two list videos were 31 s and 36 s; the 46 s hurricane list got 144 views and the worst tail retention (Ask Studio: ~19% at the end, unreliable tool).
- **What lost:** (a) abstract or non-Earth subjects: Voyager light-day 31, Everest on 5 other worlds 27, iceberg 17 (n=3 median 27) vs Earth events with a household hook (quake, volcano, hot planet) median 457+; (b) database titles ("5 Fastest Winds in the Solar System"); (c) 5-entry lists where #5 is weakest, cliff at 4 s (Ask Studio, tool unreliable); (d) burst posting: 15 videos in 8 days gave the feed no learning time per video; (e) 4 of 15 were solar-system-wide not Earth.
- **Contrast case:** "The Dinosaur Killer Isn't Earth's Biggest" retained 92.65% (295 views): when the picture is held and the claim is surprising, viewers stay; the title had no searchable name.

## 3. Strategy
- **Format:** `single-extreme` record story by default (one named event, one number, scale visible); TOP 5 secondary (<= 1 in 5) only with five comparable real entries and a visibly bigger #1. Brand name unchanged.
- **Length:** 22-30 s (our top video 26 s; ranked 31-46 s lists did worse on retention).
- **Title:** event name + number, <= 50 chars.
- **Hook:** Withheld Motion: event moving at frame 0, <= 5-word number overlay; first line starts with the subject or a verb; seam into frame 0.
- **Story:** story-playbook `micro-story` variant (GROUND -> TURN -> STEP scale -> PEAK held -> MEANING). Proposal for a new row (not edited into the playbook): `record-and-bar` = "event + record claim -> the bar it beat -> scale in frame -> held peak -> what it changes", mid-payoff 35-55%.
- **Event fast lane:** 24-48 h after a real event (quake >= M7, named storm peak, eruption episode, G3+ storm), agency imagery only, same-day rights-scout decision, number verified at the primary source or no go.
- **Cadence:** 1/day to start, 7-day review; no bursts.
- **Gate:** `viral_case` = named event + measured number + source + visible scale.
- **Success ladder:** 1.5x the 102 median -> 10K -> 100K -> 1M; claimed only when the platform shows it.

## 6. Risks
- **Rights:** CIRA/CSU loops, TV/agency, viewer clips, ESA/other-producer credits inside NASA pages; USGS pages with third-party credits. Producer-page check every source.
- **Numbers:** news sites disagree (radiation storm rank, CME speed 1,660 km/s single-source, Sinlaku figure). Only NOAA/NHC/JTWC/USGS values go on screen.
- **Stills:** SWOT and Worldview are images, not video: motion must come from our graphics; label as satellite imagery.
- **Demand uncertainty:** no per-video Shorts view counts found; the 1M case is a hypothesis to test over ~10 videos, not a claim.
- **Small sample:** n=15 with a burst-posting confound; do not over-read the diagnosis.
- **Event dependence:** quiet weeks need evergreen records; keep 5 evergreen ideas ready.
- **Radar doc** still says rankings has no radar; the orchestrator must lift that line (not edited here).
- **Misattribution trap:** never reuse viral disaster clips; fake/AI volcano video is common.
