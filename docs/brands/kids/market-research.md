**What the data says:**
1. **Shorts of 13–60 s are where new faceless kids channels break out.** Zippie Kidz, Doctor Sophia, Kidshire and VocaVoca all built audiences of roughly 1M to 10M subscribers through Shorts. Their recent long-form videos often get only 10K–40K views.
2. **The winning pattern is a learning concept plus a habit or story.** Examples: counting 1–10 while brushing teeth, colors with a tiny conflict ("Uh oh!"), opposites comparisons, and germ or sugar-bug antagonists in habit stories.
3. **Little or no dialogue travels globally.** Several of the top Shorts come from Spanish, Turkish, Indonesian or Indian channels. Language-light visuals plus music widen the audience.
4. **Seasonal timing matters.** Every major channel is publishing Halloween content this week (CoComelon, Baby Shark, Blippi, Numberblocks, Doctor Sophia). Christmas Shorts on Super Simple Songs reached 3.8M–6.9M views.
5. **Long-form value comes from compilations** (CoComelon, Baby Shark "60 Minutes", Ms Rachel 1-hour). They need a library first.

## 2. Policy and economics reality check

**Made for Kids (MFK) restrictions** (YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097, checked 2026-10-20):
- Disabled features: autoplay on home, cards and end screens, comments, notifications, personalized ads, miniplayer, Save to playlist / Watch Later, channel memberships, Super Chat, merch, and channel Posts.
- **Consequence:** subscribers do not get notified, so a new channel's reach depends almost entirely on the Shorts feed, browse/suggested, and search. End screens and cards cannot be used for internal linking, so use playlists and a series naming scheme instead.

**Quality principles** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223):
- High-quality MFK content (healthy habits, curiosity, creativity, life skills, a complete narrative with a clear lesson) gets promoted and is considered for YouTube Kids.
- Low-quality content can get limited ads, or YPP suspension at channel level. Low-quality examples include "deceptively educational", "hard-to-follow ... from mass production or auto-generation", "strange use of children's characters", and keyword stuffing.
- **This matters directly for an AI-automation studio:** every video needs a real lesson, clear audio, and a coherent story.

**Inauthentic content policy** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392, renamed 2025-07-15):
- "Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome" and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates" are ineligible to monetize.
- Recurring characters are fine, but each episode needs a distinct concept and outcome.

**COPPA:** the FTC's amended COPPA Rule took effect 2025-06-23, and full compliance was due **2026-04-22** (Davis Polk, https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect). Practical rules:
- Mark the channel as MFK. Never mislabel kids content as "not MFK" to get comments or personalized ads. That is what the FTC fined YouTube and creators over in 2019.
- Collect no child data off-platform (no "send us your drawing" email collection).

**Money:**
- AIR Media-Tech's 300-channel 2026 data (updated 2026-07-01, https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm): **Kids & Teens median RPM $0.33, median Studio CPM $1.59**, the lowest of 13 niches. Education & Science sits at $10.22 RPM.
- vidIQ (updated 2026-04-24, https://vidiq.com/blog/post/make-money-kids-youtube-channel/) says MFK CPMs are typically $1–7, with nursery rhymes and animation at about $1–3 and spikes in Q4. Shorts revenue sharing remains available to MFK channels.
- **Conclusion:** kids is a **volume and library** business. The views are achievable, but revenue per view is tiny. Keep cost per Short very low (under $5) and plan for compilations, YouTube Kids inclusion, and later licensing or merch off YouTube.

## 3. Realistic opportunity for a NEW faceless animated kids channel

- **Upside (verified):** small faceless studios with simple 2D/3D characters reached about 1M subscribers (Zippie Kidz 987K, Doctor Sophia 994K) and individual Shorts in the hundreds of millions of views within months.
- **Headwinds:**
  - Saturation from the same AI-assisted Shorts formula, mostly from non-English studios.
  - MFK removes notifications and comments.
  - Lowest RPM of any niche.
  - YouTube's 2025–26 crackdown on templated and AI-mass-produced content and on "deceptively educational" kids content.
- **Realistic first-90-day expectation (estimate, not data):** most videos will sit at 0–500 views. Growth arrives in bursts, when one Short is picked up by the Shorts feed. The first 1,000 genuine views is very achievable in week 1–2 from Shorts. Monetization (YPP) needs 1,000 subs plus 10M Shorts views in 90 days, or 4,000 watch hours. For MFK, the realistic route is the Shorts-views route.
- **Differentiators that are actually available:**
  1. One **ownable original character duo** with a consistent world. Most faceless competitors use generic babies or teeth.
  2. **Real pedagogy**: one concept per video, with a "pause and guess" beat and a repeat-and-reinforce ending.
  3. **Calmer pacing** that parents prefer. Ms Rachel's success suggests parents reward slower, teacher-like content. That is an inference, **unverified** as causal.
  4. **Language-light** design so the same video can be dubbed into Spanish and Arabic later.

## 4. Recommendations

**Age band:** **2–5 years (preschool)**, with core visuals built for ages 3–4. All the top-performing comps target this band (counting 1–10, colors, opposites, routines). For ages 6+, simple science works better as general-audience content, not MFK.

**Format mix (week 1–8):**
- **70% Shorts** (15–45 s, vertical, music plus one sung or spoken line): counting, colors, opposites, habit micro-stories.
- **25% long-form, 2–4 min**: one song or one story with a full narrative arc, built for YouTube Kids and search.
- **5% compilations, 10–30 min**: start in week 2–3 once 6 or more long-form pieces exist. Aim for 60-min compilations by month 3, since CTV/TV viewers watch long blocks.

**Durations:**
- Shorts: 20–35 s (comps' megahits run 0:13–0:50).
- Songs: 2:00–3:30.
- Stories: 3–5 min.
- Compilations: 12, 30 and 60 min.

**Animation style:**
- Soft-rounded 2D or 2.5D (vector or Rive/Lottie-style, or toon-shaded 3D).
- High-saturation primary palette, slow camera, big readable objects, a single focal object per frame.
- Sound effects on every action, plus a 1-beat "uh-oh!" conflict and a "yay!" resolution.
- No fast cuts. Captions in large font for parents.

**Recurring character concept direction (original IP, trademark-search before launch):**
- **"Tiko & Pip"**: Tiko is a small, round, curious turtle with a backpack that holds "today's lesson object". Pip is a tiny firefly whose glow changes color and lights up the correct answer, which also works as the "pause and guess" device.
- Supporting cast is added slowly: Grandma Owl for bedtime and stories, and the Sugar Bugs as soft, non-scary antagonists for habit videos.
- Each episode = one concept + one small problem + one resolution, which keeps outcomes varied and avoids inauthentic-content flags.
- Avoid any resemblance to CoComelon (JJ), Bluey, or Pinkfong characters.

**Upload frequency:**
- Days 1–14: 1 Short per day plus 2 long-form per week.
- Later: 1–2 Shorts per day if quality holds, plus 2 long-form and 1 compilation per week.

## 5. Growth plan to the first 1,000 genuine views

1. **Shorts first.** The Shorts feed does not depend on subscribers, which matters because MFK disables notifications. Publish 14 Shorts in 14 days, then double down on whichever concept gets the highest "viewed vs swiped" rate.
2. **Titles use the words parents actually type:**
   - Pattern: "Learn to Count 1–10 with …", "Learn Colors with …", "Which One Is BIG? …", "Brush Your Teeth Song | Good Habits for Kids".
   - Put the concept first and the character second ("Tiko & Pip" goes at the end).
   - Avoid keyword stuffing, which is a low-quality principle.
3. **Thumbnails (long-form):** one big character face with an emotion, one object, 2–3 colors, and 0–3 words ("1–10!", "BIG or small?").
4. **Hook in the first 1 s:** start on motion or conflict, never on a logo or title card. For example, a pumpkin rolls away ("Uh-oh!"), or Pip blinks red, yellow, then blue.
5. **Seasonal riding:** Halloween (now to Oct 31), then Thanksgiving, winter clothes (Kidshire's "Winter Clothes" has 1.6B views), and Christmas.
6. **Playlists** per concept (Counting, Colors, Opposites, Habits) to replace the end screens that MFK disables.
7. **Genuine external sharing:** parent and teacher communities (Pinterest pins of free printable coloring pages that link to the video, and teacher resource groups), always following each community's rules. No paid fake views and no sub-for-sub.
8. **Measure:** the Shorts "viewed vs swiped away" percentage and long-form average view duration. Kill formats under 50% viewed after 3 attempts.

## 6. Weighted idea-scoring model (used in ideas.json)

Each factor is scored 1–5:
- `demand`: evidence of views for the topic.
- `competitionGap`: room for a new entrant.
- `visualPotential`: suitability for animation.
- `productionEase`: 5 = cheapest and fastest.
- `policyRisk`: **5 = highest risk**.

**Score = 0.30·demand + 0.20·competitionGap + 0.20·visualPotential + 0.15·productionEase + 0.15·(6 − policyRisk)**, with a maximum of 5.0. Ship ideas scoring ≥ 3.8 first.

Kids-specific modifier: an idea scores policyRisk ≥ 3 automatically if it contains anything that could be read as scary, a stranger or safety topic, food or medical advice, or a well-known third-party character.
