## 2. What keeps preschoolers watching (evidence), healthy vs. to avoid

### Healthy engagement (use)
1. **Participation pauses (Blue's Clues).** Question, a visible 2 to 4 s pause, then the answer. Anderson, Bryant, Wilder, Santomero, Williams, Crawley (2002, Media Psychology) compared regular viewers to matched non-viewers and found positive effects on cognitive development; correlational, so treat as supportive, not proof. Repeating an episode helps because early repetitions raise attention to the educational content (Crawley et al.). We already do "guess, pause, reveal" (episode-recipe: `after` 2.0 s).
2. **Direct address and a responsive character.** Toddlers learn less from video than from a live person (the "video deficit", Anderson & Pempek 2005); responsiveness on a screen alone did NOT reliably fix it for toddlers (Troseth et al. 2018, "Let's Chat", 176 toddlers), while a co-viewing adult helps transfer. So: talk to "you/friend", look into the lens at the pause, and write lines a parent can repeat ("Can you point to red?"). Ms Rachel's formula (simple repeated songs, parentese) is explained by a University of South Wales researcher as a language-and-social-skill method; it is an expert opinion piece, not a trial.
3. **Slow, calm pacing.** Lillard & Peterson (2011, Pediatrics 128:644) randomised 60 four-year-olds to 9 minutes of a fast-paced cartoon (SpongeBob), a slower educational cartoon (Caillou) or drawing: the fast-cartoon group did worse on executive-function tasks right afterwards. Only immediate effects were measured, 4-year-olds only, one fast show; still a good reason to keep our rule (a visible event every 3 to 6 s, never a cut storm, voice 110 to 130 wpm).
4. **Predictable structure and repetition.** Same opening, same segment order, same goodbye. Predictability gives toddlers the confidence to join in. The key word at least 3 times per episode.
5. **Songs and a jingle.** One short signature jingle and one recap song per episode, simple rhythm, the target words sung. Songs carry the "can you say it with me?" call-and-response.
6. **Celebration and praise of effort.** Cheers, sparkles and "Great job, friend!" for trying, not for finishing fast. Soft sparkles only, no flashing above 3 Hz.
7. **Colour and contrast, big faces.** Bright sky, one focal object per scene, big readable characters; this also makes the thumbnail work.
8. **Distinct character voices and a catchphrase ritual.** Leda narrates, Tiko (Puck) leads, Pip chirps. A repeated catchphrase plus a tiny action ("Boing! Boing!") gives children a role.
9. **A gentle "next time" teaser** (curiosity, not suspense): one happy, concrete image of the next topic.

### Avoid (manipulative or low-quality by YouTube's own list)
- Cliffhangers that create worry, sad or scary hooks, characters "leaving forever", guilt ("Pip will be sad if you don't subscribe").
- Autoplay-style endless looping structure, pressure countdowns, "don't go!", variable-reward loot or slot-machine reveals.
- Flashing, rapid cuts, loud stingers, jump scares.
- Keyword-stuffed titles, clickbait thumbnails (YouTube lists "sensational or misleading" and "keyword stuffing" as low quality), titles that promise learning the video does not deliver ("deceptively educational").
- Product placement, toys, unboxing, brand logos, "heavily promotional" content; encouraging buying or accumulating.
- Asking children to act on the platform (click, comment, ring the bell, go to a link). Ask a grown-up instead.
- Mass-produced "hard to follow" episodes without a clear beginning, middle and end: each episode needs its own tiny story and payoff.

## 3. YouTube Made-for-Kids rules that constrain us

Sources: YouTube Help "Watching made for kids content" (support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097), "Best practices for kids & family content" (support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223), content policies for YouTube Kids (support.google.com/youtube/answer/10938174), FTC/YouTube settlement coverage (FPF, TechCrunch 2019-09-04).
- **Disabled on MFK videos:** cards and end screens, comments, notifications (bell), autoplay on home, watermark, save to playlist / Watch Later, miniplayer, memberships, donations, live chat, merchandise, personalised ads. **On MFK channels:** memberships, notifications, posts, comments. (Subscribing itself is not on the disabled list; confirm once in Studio.)
  - Consequence: no clickable "next video" wall, no pinned comment, no "ring the bell". Our wall is a picture, not a button, so label it for grown-ups and keep it out of the 'tap me' look.
- **COPPA (FTC settlement, 2019, USD 170M):** creators are responsible for marking content correctly; no personalised ads/data collection from kids on MFK. We collect nothing and run no off-platform links, no "visit our website", no asking for names, ages or comments.
- **Quality principles (YouTube):** high quality = looking after yourself and others, learning and curiosity, creativity and play, life skills with a complete narrative and takeaway, shows the world (diverse characters). Low quality = heavily promotional, negative behaviours, deceptively educational, hard to follow (no clear beginning/middle/end), sensational/misleading incl. keyword stuffing, strange use of known characters. The page lists no rule on calls to action or overstimulation, so our CTA rule below is a judgement, set conservative: it is on the child-safe side of every listed principle.
- **Compliant CTA = in-video only, short, character-led, addressed to the grown-up, no pressure, no promise or reward for subscribing, no sad reaction if they do not.** Never mention comments, bell, links, other apps or merch. No "AI-generated" text in descriptions (studio rule) and no brand/third-party characters.
- Playlists still work for the *creator*: publish "Sunny Hill: All Episodes (in order)" and put it in every description (grown-ups read it; kids do not).

## 4. Series format spec (every episode, 16:9, 2:30 to 3:00 total)

### Signature opening (0:00 to 0:07)
**Owner 2026-11-08 (O5/O-C3, approved):** every episode starts with a cold open of at most 3 s (the first payoff action in a moving 3D frame); the signature opening below follows it, moved after the first payoff action or shortened (kit change goes to the pipeline engineer). Trailer test (O-S4) = LATER, second.
| Time | What | Line |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:01.5 | Motion in 0.3 s: sunny sky, hill rises, Pip flies in | jingle hook (4 notes, major, soft) |
| 0:01.5 to 0:04.5 | Title card "Sunny Hill" + Tiko & Pip, sparkles | sung: "Sunny Hill, Sunny Hill, come and play!" |
| 0:04.5 to 0:07 | Episode badge "Ep 3 · Colours" slides in; Tiko waves | Tiko: "Hello, friend! It's Tiko!" (Pip chirps) |
First 3 s must already show the topic object (episode hook rule). Opening never exceeds 7 s so episodes do not feel like a delay.

### Fixed segments
1. **Hello + today's goal (0:07 to 0:25):** catchphrase ritual (below), name the topic once, invite a movement ("point with me").
2. **Learn it, 3 to 5 rounds (0:25 to ~1:50):** guess, pause 2 s, reveal, "say it with me" pause 1.2 to 1.6 s, repeat. One structure per episode; different game each episode (see `episode-recipe.md` section 0).
3. **Story beat "wait..." (~1:50 to 2:20):** Pip finds the surprise; happy resolution in the same scene.
4. **Do it together + body move (2:20 to 2:35):** clap, jump, trace in the air.
5. **Ending (section 5), about 25 s.**
Total ≈ 2:35 to 3:00.

### Ritual and catchphrase
- Catchphrase (Tiko + child): **"Up the hill, up the hill... Ready, friend? Boing!"** Tiko and Pip jump together on "Boing!"; opening of segment 2 and again at the story beat. Same words every episode, so toddlers can join. Goodbye catchphrase: **"See you on the hill!"** with the wave.
- Ritual: each episode ends with the three of us (Tiko, Pip, child) doing the "hill clap": two claps and a jump, on screen, 3 s.
- Pip's signature: a two-note chirp that returns every time something is found.

### Numbering and titling
- Number in order of upload, never reuse: `Ep 1 ... Ep N`. Titles: `Sunny Hill Ep 3: Colours with Tiko & Pip | Learn Colors for Toddlers`. Max 70 chars, topic keyword first after Ep number, no stuffing, no all caps, no emojis in the topic text.
- Playlists: "Sunny Hill: All Episodes (in order)", plus topic playlists (Numbers, Colours, Shapes, Animals, Feelings, Habits).
- Description (parent-facing): one line what the child learns, 2 to 3 "try at home" ideas, link to the playlist, "Made for kids". No AI-generation text, no "subscribe!" shouting.
- Seasons: 12 episodes = Season 1. Keep the recap wall to the latest 3 plus 1 older classic (section 5).

### Thumbnail style rules (1280x720)
Bright sky, Tiko + Pip big faces smiling (never worried), ONE object, max 2 words ("Colours!"), the episode badge ("EP 3") in a rounded corner chip, same corner every episode so the grid reads as a series. Plain navy text with orange accent, rounded font, minimum 3x contrast. Never fake arrows, never red circles, never a face of a real child, no "NEW!!!".

## 5. The ending formula (about 25 s, always in this order)

| Time (from end) | Segment | Content and exact lines |
|---|---|---|
| -25 to -13 (12 s) | **Recap song** | Short sung reprise of the target words with a bounce (e.g. colours: "Red, blue, yellow, green"), each word shown once with its object, Tiko and Pip dance. Narrator: "Let's sing what we learned!" |
| -13 to -8 (5 s) | **Next-time teaser** | One happy peek: the next topic object pops from behind the hill. Narrator: "Next time on Sunny Hill: Shapes!" Pip bounces, no suspense, no worry. |
| -8 to -2 (6 s) | **Gentle ask, character-led, to grown-ups** | Pip waves; narrator voice: "Grown-ups, if you liked playing with us, you can subscribe so we can play again!" Pip then: *chirp chirp* (happy). No promise, no consequence, no sad face. |
| -2 to 0 | **See you** | Tiko: "See you on the hill!" Hill clap and jump. |

**"Watch more" wall (in-video, because end screens are off on MFK):** embedded during the ask, from -10 s to the end, as a soft picture row at the bottom of the frame: 3 tiles (the previous 2 episodes + one classic), each with the episode badge (Ep 2, Ep 1...), titled by number only. Spoken: "Grown-ups, find Ep 1 and Ep 2 in our Sunny Hill playlist." The tiles are not buttons: no tap affordance, no arrow, no "click here". Rotate which episodes show; Ep 1 has no older wall, show "Coming next: Ep 2".
Rules: ask appears once; never in the first 90 s; never mentions comments, bell, link, notifications, merch or channel growth; never says "like"; no audio spike under it; total ending ≤ 25 s so it does not read as a delay. Update `prelint` allow-list for this exact ask (the current rule bans "subscribe" lines) and add the ask to `episode.json` as a dedicated scene `outro` so it is the same in every episode.
